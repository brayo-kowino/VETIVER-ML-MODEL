Real Failure Data Collection Guide

Standard column definitions, accepted values, and field instructions for collecting real negative (Suitability = 0) trial records.

Why this matters: The current model is trained on 91 field success records and 16 engineered failure boundaries. To make predictions trustworthy across East African conditions, we need at least 30–50 real, documented failure cases from published literature and field reports. This sheet standardises what to collect so it merges directly into training with zero reformatting.
1. Exact CSV Column Headers

Copy this row exactly as the first row of your Google Sheet. Column names must match character-for-character including capitalisation and spacing.

Location(country/region), Soil type/structure, Soil density, climate, watering regime, salinity, Duration since planting, Root length(cm), Application, Reference, Failure mode, Failure cause details, Suitability
2. Column-by-Column Field Guide
Column Name What to Record Example Values
Location(country/region) Country and specific region, county, or city where the trial was conducted. Kenya (Kitengela), Ethiopia (Tigray), Tanzania (Dodoma Region), Uganda (Mbarara)
Soil type/structure Soil texture or classification exactly as described in the paper or report. Do not simplify — copy the original term. Black cotton soils, Saline-sodic soil, Shallow rocky laterite, Sandy clay loam
Soil density Bulk density or compaction description if reported. Use NR if not reported. 1.8 g/cm³, Highly compacted, NR
climate Climate zone or rainfall regime of the site as described in the source. Semi-arid, Arid/hyper-arid, Tropical dry, Semi-arid tropical
watering regime Water availability during the trial. For failures due to drought, record clearly (e.g. "No irrigation / rainfed drought year"). Rainfed, No irrigation, Seasonal drought, Dry season only
salinity Soil salinity reading or description. Use actual EC value in dS/m if available. Key failure threshold: EC > 12–20 dS/m. ECe > 20 dS/m, EC = 22 dS/m, Hyper-saline, NR
Duration since planting How long the trial ran before it was reported as a failure or terminated. 3 months, 6 months, 12 weeks, NR
Root length(cm) Root penetration depth observed. Will be near zero or NR for failure cases. Record 0 if roots completely failed to penetrate. 0, < 5, NR
Application What the planting was intended to achieve at the site. Slope stabilization, Erosion control, Mine rehabilitation, Land restoration
Reference Full citation of the paper, report, or field observation. Use author-year format. For unpublished field trials, write the team name and date. Njoroge et al. (2023), JKUAT Field Trial Report Oct 2024, KeNHA Site Audit (2022)
Failure modeNEW Short category label for the type of failure. Must use one of the accepted values from Section 3 below. Establishment failure, Salinity kill, Drought kill, Bedrock refusal
Failure cause detailsNEW One clear sentence describing the documented reason the site failed, in your own words based on the source. Plants failed to root due to impenetrable laterite hardpan at 8 cm depth. High salinity (EC 22 dS/m) caused complete desiccation within 6 weeks of planting.
SuitabilityALWAYS = 0 The machine learning label. Every failure record must be exactly the number zero (0). No other value. 0
3. Accepted Failure Mode Labels

Use only these exact labels in the Failure mode column. Consistent values are critical for model training and analysis.

Failure Mode Label What It Means Key Trigger
Establishment failure Slips germinated or were planted but never successfully rooted into the soil Soil compaction, hard substrate
Complete die-off All or nearly all plants died during the trial period Extreme heat, toxicity, drought
Root death Roots were initially present but collapsed or rotted before anchoring Waterlogging, anoxic sludge
Poor survival Less than 30% plant survival recorded at end of trial Combined stresses, marginal conditions
Growth stunting Plants remained alive but showed zero meaningful root development for slope protection Nutrient toxicity, heavy metals
Salinity kill Failure directly and explicitly attributed to high electrical conductivity EC > 12–20 dS/m
Drought kill Failure directly attributed to water deficit during establishment No rainfall, no irrigation
Bedrock refusal Roots physically unable to penetrate substrate; effective depth < 15 cm Rock, hardpan, laterite
Frost kill Failure due to freezing temperatures destroying plant tissue Temperatures below −10°C
Toxicity failure Failure due to heavy metal accumulation, acid sulfates, or chemical contamination Mine tailings, acid soil, agrochemicals

5. Where to Find Real Failure Records

Tell your Literature Specialist to search specifically for these keywords and sources:

Search Keywords (use in Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, ResearchGate):

  • "Vetiver failed to establish" / "poor survival" / "plant mortality"
  • "Chrysopogon zizanioides unsuitable" / "trial discontinued"
  • "Vetiver salinity tolerance limit" / "EC threshold die-off"
  • "Vetiver drought stress mortality" / "water deficit establishment"
  • "Vetiver mine tailings failure" / "acid soil toxicity"

Priority Institutional Sources for East Africa:

  • TVNI — The Vetiver Network International grey literature and site reports
  • KALRO — Kenya Agricultural and Livestock Research Organisation field reports
  • EIAR — Ethiopian Institute of Agricultural Research annual reviews
  • TARI — Tanzania Agricultural Research Institute trial reports
  • KeNHA / KURA — Kenya road embankment slope failure post-mortem audits
  • JKUAT — Internal departmental field trial records and student theses
Minimum collection target: 30–50 real, documented failure records before the next model retraining session. The current engineered boundaries will be replaced by real observed failures once this data is ready. Every additional failure row improves the model's ability to protect real sites from wrong advice.
