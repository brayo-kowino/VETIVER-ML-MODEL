# VETIVER GRASS (CHRYSOPOGON ZIZANIOIDES) BIOENGINEERING FAILURE REGISTER & MITIGATION MANUAL

## Overview & Engineering Intent
While Vetiver grass (*Chrysopogon zizanioides*) is exceptionally resilient and widely deployed for slope stabilization, erosion control, and tailings remediation, bioengineering failures occur when physical, geotechnical, hydrological, or physiological exclusion boundaries are breached. This document compiles 60 scientifically documented failure cases across the globe and East Africa, pairing each failure mode with published peer-reviewed engineer and scientist countermeasures.

---

## Biomechanical & Geotechnical Limit States Summary
1. **Slip Plane Depth Boundary:** Vetiver root reinforcement creates a dense tensile anchor envelope primarily down to 1.5–2.0 m (rarely 3.0 m in deep porous soils). Deep-seated rotational or translational slip surfaces deeper than 2.0 m exceed the biological shear envelope and require structural precursors (soil nails, micropiles, rock anchors).
2. **Hydraulic Shear Velocity Boundary:** Juvenile hedgerows (<6 months) fail by hydrodynamic uprooting at flow velocities >2.0–2.5 m/s. Once mature (root crown tillering >6 months), hedgerows resist tractive shear stresses up to 300–400 N/m² and flows up to 3.5–5.0 m/s.
3. **Chemical / Salinity Boundary:** The physiological threshold for Vetiver survival is electrical conductivity EC ≤ 16–20 dS/m and SAR ≤ 25. Hypersaline substrates require gypsum pre-treatment (5–12 t/ha) and gradual saline nursery hardening.
4. **Light & Heliophyte Boundary:** Vetiver is an obligate C4 heliophyte requiring at least 40–50% full sunlight. Overstory canopy shading >60% causes etiolation, tillering cessation, and eventual biomass collapse.
5. **Anoxic Submergence Limit:** Foliage leaf crowns must maintain contact with atmospheric oxygen. Continuous total submergence of crowns exceeding 21–30 days induces anoxic crown rotting.
6. **Thermal & Elevation Boundary:** Tropical Vetiver cessation of active growth occurs when sustained mean temperatures drop <10–12°C or elevations exceed 2,200 m in East African highlands.

## Detailed Case Register (60 Empirical Failure Cases & Countermeasures)

### Case 1: Guangdong, China – Deep-seated rotational slip
- **Application:** Cut-slope highway stabilization
- **Substrate & Soil Type:** Decomposed Granite (Density: 1.65 g/cm³, Salinity: 0.2 dS/m)
- **Climate & Watering:** Humid Subtropical | Monsoon (1,700 mm/yr)
- **Duration & Root Length:** 8 mo | Root depth: 180 cm
- **Citation / Reference:** Hengchaovanich (1998)
- **Failure Mechanism Details:** Shear plane occurred at 3.5 m depth, exceeding the 1.8 m effective root anchoring zone.
- **Published Scientist Mitigation:** Hengchaovanich (1998) specified: Bioengineering cannot supersede deep structural mechanics; slopes with slip circles >1.5–2.0 m require passive soil nailing or rock anchors combined with subsurface horizontal drains, using vetiver strictly for surficial veneer stability.
- **Geotechnical Exclusion Boundary:** `Unsuitable for deep slope failure without structural anchoring`

### Case 2: Queensland, Australia – Hydraulic uprooting
- **Application:** Floodway channel stabilization
- **Substrate & Soil Type:** Silt-Clay Alluvium (Density: 1.45 g/cm³, Salinity: 1.1 dS/m)
- **Climate & Watering:** Subtropical Semi-arid | Floodplain runoff
- **Duration & Root Length:** 2 mo | Root depth: 35 cm
- **Citation / Reference:** Truong et al. (2002)
- **Failure Mechanism Details:** High-energy overland flow (>3.5 m/s) struck juvenile plants before adequate root anchorage developed.
- **Published Scientist Mitigation:** Truong et al. (2002) recommended: Plants must be established for ≥6 months before exposure to design velocities >2.0 m/s; where early flash flows threaten, anchor rows with pinned high-tensile biodegradable jute/poly netting over hedgerow crowns.
- **Geotechnical Exclusion Boundary:** `Unsuitable for high-velocity early flow (<6 months establishment)`

### Case 3: Queensland, Australia – Osmotic desiccation / mortality
- **Application:** Mine tailings rehabilitation
- **Substrate & Soil Type:** Sodic Clay (Density: 1.58 g/cm³, Salinity: 18.5 dS/m)
- **Climate & Watering:** Tropical / Subtropical | Drip irrigation
- **Duration & Root Length:** 12 wk | Root depth: 25 cm
- **Citation / Reference:** Truong (1994)
- **Failure Mechanism Details:** EC exceeded survival threshold (>16 dS/m) during active germination/transplanting.
- **Published Scientist Mitigation:** Truong (1994) documented: Pre-treat sodic substrates with gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O) at 5–10 t/ha to displace exchangeable Na+; harden slips in containerized polybags with gradual saline conditioning before field transplanting into soils exceeding 10 dS/m.
- **Geotechnical Exclusion Boundary:** `Unsuitable under extreme hypersaline conditions`

### Case 4: Java, Indonesia – Etiolation and biomass collapse
- **Application:** Agroforestry hillside terracing
- **Substrate & Soil Type:** Volcanic Clay Loam (Density: 1.25 g/cm³, Salinity: 0.1 dS/m)
- **Climate & Watering:** Tropical Rainforest | High rainfall (>3,000 mm/yr)
- **Duration & Root Length:** 18 mo | Root depth: 60 cm
- **Citation / Reference:** Utomo et al. (2000)
- **Failure Mechanism Details:** Heavy canopy closure (>75% shading) from adjacent multi-tier agroforestry timber trees.
- **Published Scientist Mitigation:** Utomo et al. (2000) concluded: Vetiver is an obligate C4 light-demanding heliophyte requiring minimum 40–50% full sunlight; agroforestry alleys must enforce bi-annual silvicultural canopy pruning and lopping to sustain hedgerow vigor.
- **Geotechnical Exclusion Boundary:** `Unsuitable under continuous dense tree canopy shade`

### Case 5: Madagascar – Severe crown defoliation & die-off
- **Application:** Agricultural contour bunding
- **Substrate & Soil Type:** Ferralsol (Laterite) (Density: 1.52 g/cm³, Salinity: 0.1 dS/m)

- **Geotechnical Exclusion Boundary:** `Unsuitable in unfenced, heavily grazed communal landscapes`

### Case 6: KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa – Basal flume undermining

### Case 10: Limpopo, South Africa – Metal phytotoxicity & root stunting
